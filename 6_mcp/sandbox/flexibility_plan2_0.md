# Flexibility Plan 2.0: Beginner Path to Butterfly Kicks and B-Twists

![Dynamic warm-up example](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1517836357463-d25dfeac3438?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

## Goal
Build beginner-friendly flexibility, mobility, and body control so you can safely progress toward **butterfly kicks** and eventually **B-twists**. These tricks require a mix of hamstring flexibility, hip mobility, back and shoulder openness, balance, leg swing mechanics, and rotational coordination. As a starter, your focus should be on consistent basics rather than forcing advanced moves early.

---

## Before You Start
- Train flexibility **4 to 5 days per week**.
- Practice trick foundations **2 to 3 days per week**.
- Always warm up before stretching.
- Stretch to mild discomfort, **not pain**.
- Progress slowly; good form matters more than range.
- If you feel sharp pain in the hips, knees, back, or hamstrings, stop.

A good timeline for a beginner is **8 to 12 weeks** of steady work before expecting major technical progress on butterfly kick height or B-twist readiness.

---

## What You Need for Butterfly Kicks and B-Twists
### 1. Hamstring flexibility
You need enough range to lift the chest and swing the kicking leg without rounding excessively.

### 2. Hip mobility
Open hips help with the long, sweeping line of a butterfly kick and support cleaner takeoff mechanics.

### 3. Adductor and groin flexibility
This helps with wider stances, deeper setup positions, and reduced tension during swings.

### 4. Thoracic spine and shoulder mobility
Butterfly kicks and B-twists look better and feel smoother when the chest can open and the arms can swing freely.

### 5. Glute and core strength
Flexibility without control is unstable. You need active mobility, not just passive stretching.

## Exercise Examples

## 1. Leg Swings
![Leg swings example](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/Front_leg_swing_exercise.jpg/640px-Front_leg_swing_exercise.jpg)
**How:** Hold a wall, swing one leg forward and back, then side to side.  
**Sets:** 2 sets of 10 to 15 each direction per leg.  
**Why:** Builds dynamic hamstring and hip mobility needed for kick height.

## 2. Deep Lunge Hip Flexor Stretch
![Lunge stretch example](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1518611012118-696072aa579a?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
**How:** Step into a long lunge, lower the back knee, keep chest tall, gently press hips forward.  
**Hold:** 30 to 45 seconds each side for 2 to 3 rounds.  
**Why:** Helps open the front of the hips for cleaner setup positions.

## 3. Standing Hamstring Stretch
![Hamstring stretch example](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1544367567-0f2fcb009e0b?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
**How:** Place one heel on a low surface, keep the back straight, hinge forward from the hips.  
**Hold:** 30 seconds each leg for 2 rounds.  
**Why:** Improves the long leg line needed in butterfly kick swings.

## 4. Straddle Fold / Pancake Stretch
![Straddle stretch example](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1506126613408-eca07ce68773?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
**How:** Sit wide, toes up, hinge forward with a flat back.  
**Hold:** 30 to 60 seconds for 2 to 3 rounds.  
**Why:** Builds adductor and hamstring flexibility for wide, controlled movement.

## 5. Cossack Squats
![Cossack squat example](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1518459031867-a89b944bffe4?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
**How:** Take a wide stance, shift into one side while the other leg stays straighter, alternate sides.  
**Sets:** 2 to 3 sets of 6 to 8 reps per side.  
**Why:** Improves lateral hip mobility, adductor strength, and control.

## 6. Frog Stretch
![Frog stretch example](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1518310383802-640c2de311b2?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
**How:** Kneel on all fours, widen the knees, keep shins out, gently sink the hips back.  
**Hold:** 20 to 40 seconds for 2 rounds.  
**Why:** Opens the groin and inner thighs.

## 7. Glute Bridges
![Glute bridge example](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1517838277536-f5f99be501cd?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
**How:** Lie on your back, feet planted, drive through heels, lift hips, squeeze glutes.  
**Sets:** 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps.  
**Why:** Supports hip drive and lower-body control.

## 8. Side Leg Raises
![Side leg raise example](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1517343985841-f8b2d66e010b?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
**How:** Stand tall or lie on your side and lift the top leg with control.  
**Sets:** 2 to 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps per side.  
**Why:** Builds active hip range and kicking strength.

## 9. Superman Holds
![Superman hold example](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1517838277536-f5f99be501cd?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
**How:** Lie face down, lift arms and legs slightly, keep neck neutral.  
**Hold:** 15 to 25 seconds for 3 rounds.  
**Why:** Helps with arch control and chest openness during trick mechanics.

## 10. Hollow Body Hold
![Core training example](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1518611012118-696072aa579a?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
**How:** Lie on your back, press lower back down, lift shoulders and legs slightly.  
**Hold:** 15 to 30 seconds for 3 rounds.  
**Why:** Creates body tension for better aerial control.

## Butterfly Kick Progression

### Phase 1: Foundation
- Practice low sweeping leg motions.
- Work on a strong chest-down but long-spine position.
- Learn the step-behind entry and circular arm swing.
- Drill balance on the takeoff leg.

### Phase 2: Low Butterfly Kicks
- Start on soft ground or mats.
- Focus on making the kick travel sideways rather than upward.
- Keep the chest open and eyes tracking the direction of travel.
- Aim for smoothness before height.

### Phase 3: Improve Height and Line
- Increase swing speed.
- Push through the standing leg more aggressively.
- Keep both legs extended longer.
- Film yourself to check whether your torso collapses or your legs bend too early.

---

## B-Twist Readiness Plan
Do **not** rush to full B-twists. First make sure you have:
- A comfortable butterfly kick on both preferred and non-preferred sides.
- Good jump turn mechanics.
- Strong arm swing timing.
- Enough confidence leaving the ground sideways.

### Beginner B-Twist Prep Drills
1. **Butterfly kick to raised landing**  
   Helps you feel lift from the setup.
2. **Cheat 360s**  
   Teaches turning with takeoff commitment.
3. **Pop 180 and 360 jump drills**  
   Builds aerial awareness.
4. **B-kick with stronger takeoff arm swing**  
   Bridges the gap between butterfly kick and B-twist mechanics.
5. **Trampoline or mat work with a qualified coach if available**  
   Best for safety and confidence.

---

## 8-Week Starter Progression

### Weeks 1 to 2
- Learn all stretches and drills.
- Do not force depth.
- Practice butterfly kick entries only.

### Weeks 3 to 4
- Increase hold times slightly.
- Add more active flexibility work.
- Begin low butterfly kicks.

### Weeks 5 to 6
- Improve kick line and landing control.
- Add jump turns and cheat 360 drills.
- Start combining flexibility and power work.

### Weeks 7 to 8
- Refine butterfly kick height and smoothness.
- Increase twisting prep only if landings are consistent.
- If available, begin assisted B-twist drills in a safe environment.

## Important Safety Notes
- Train on grass, mats, or another forgiving surface when learning.
- Avoid maximal twisting attempts when tired.
- Never sacrifice knee alignment for deeper stretches.
- Film your practice or work with a coach if possible.
- For B-twists especially, spotting and safe progressions matter a lot.

---

## How to Know You’re Improving
You are on the right path if:
- Leg swings rise higher with less effort.
- You can hinge forward more without rounding hard.
- Your butterfly kick feels smoother and less forced.
- You can keep legs straighter during swings.
- Your landings are more balanced.
- Twist prep drills feel less scary and more controlled.

---

## Final Advice
For a beginner, the fastest route to butterfly kicks and B-twists is not trying the hardest version immediately. It is building **mobility, active flexibility, strength, and clean entry mechanics**. If you stay consistent for 2 months, your body will move better and your technique work will become much easier. Prioritize control first, then amplitude, then twist progression.
