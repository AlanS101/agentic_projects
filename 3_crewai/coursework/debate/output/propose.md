I strongly support the motion: **there should be strict laws to regulate LLMs**.

LLMs are not ordinary software tools. They can generate persuasive misinformation at scale, assist fraud, amplify bias, leak sensitive data, and be used to automate harmful activity faster than any human can respond. When a technology has the power to influence public opinion, shape decisions, and be misused globally within seconds, leaving it largely unregulated is reckless.

Strict laws are necessary for three reasons:

**First, public safety.**  
Without legal limits, companies can deploy models that are inaccurate, manipulative, or dangerously misused. We already see fake medical advice, deepfake-style text scams, and automated phishing. Regulation can require safety testing, risk assessments, and clear accountability before release.

**Second, accountability.**  
Right now, when an LLM causes harm, responsibility is often blurred between developer, deployer, and user. Strict laws force clear standards: who is liable, what data can be used, and what safeguards must exist. If no one is legally responsible, then victims are left without protection.

**Third, trust and fairness.**  
LLMs are trained on vast amounts of data and can reproduce discrimination, privacy violations, and copyright abuse. Strong regulation can enforce transparency, data protection, and anti-bias measures so these systems serve the public rather than exploit it.

The argument against regulation is usually that it might slow innovation. But that is a false choice. We regulate medicines, aircraft, and financial systems not to stop progress, but to make progress safe and trustworthy. The same principle applies here. Innovation without guardrails does not create freedom — it creates harm.

So the issue is not whether LLMs should exist. They already do. The issue is whether society will control their risks before those risks control us. For that reason, **strict laws to regulate LLMs are not optional — they are essential**.
